# CS360: Mobile Architecture and Programming

### Weight-Tracking Application

For this project, I developed a weight-tracking app for Android that allows users to record their weight, view previous entries, and set a goal weight. The app also includes user accounts, a database to store everything, and SMS notifications for when a user reaches their goal. I wanted to keep the app pretty simple since the whole point is to make tracking your weight easier, not give users a bunch of extra stuff they don't need.

### User Interface and Design

The app needed a login and registration screen, a way to view previous weight entries, and options for adding new weights and setting a goal. I used a grid to display the dates and weights so users could easily look back at their progress. I tried to keep everything straightforward and make sure users wouldn't have to go through several screens just to record their weight. I think the interface turned out pretty well for what the app was supposed to do, although there are definitely things I would change if I continued working on it.

### Development Process

I worked on the app a little at a time, starting with the interface and then adding the functionality behind it. Getting the screens designed was one thing, but making everything actually work with the database was another. I found it easier to work on one feature at a time and test it before moving on to the next. That way, when something stopped working, I had a better idea of what caused it instead of having to dig through the entire project.

### Testing

I used the Android emulator to test the app as I worked on it. I made sure I could create an account, log in, add weight entries, and have everything save correctly. I also checked that the goal weight could be updated and that the app displayed the correct information when I returned to a screen. One thing I've learned from this project and others is that just because something works once doesn't mean it's going to work every time. Testing different inputs and going back through features after making changes helped me catch problems I probably wouldn't have noticed otherwise.

### Challenges

I think one of the bigger challenges was getting the database and interface to work together correctly. It's easy enough to put a button on a screen, but making sure it actually does what it's supposed to and updates everything correctly can be a little more frustrating. I had to go back and troubleshoot different parts of the app as I added features. I ended up breaking things down and testing them individually, which made it a lot easier to figure out what was going wrong.

### Skills and Accomplishments

I think I did particularly well with the database side of the project. Getting the app to save and retrieve information for different users, along with their weight entries and goals, was probably the most important part of making the app functional.

Before this course, I hadn't done any Android development, so getting a working application together was a good experience. I also have a better understanding of how much goes into building even a relatively simple mobile app. There's a lot more involved than just designing a few screens, especially when you have to manage user data and make sure everything works together. I could definitely improve the interface and add more features, but I'm happy with how the app turned out for this project.
